# Getting to 50+ Google reviews

Expands item 1 of the priority list in [SOCIAL.md](SOCIAL.md) and §2 of [OFFSITE.md](OFFSITE.md). This is the highest-return action available to the business and the one that needs the least new work — the conversations it depends on are already happening.

**Where we stand:** rated **4.6** (store) and **5.0** (factory) — better than nearly every competitor. **Volume is the problem, not sentiment.** Hercules Group has 192 reviews, S&H Laser Signs 60. Ratings that good with volume that low are the clearest sign of a business that has simply never asked.

**The maths.** Going from 14 to 50 means about **36 net new reviews**. Google silently filters perhaps 15–20% of legitimate ones, so aim to generate **45–50 posted**. At a realistic 25–35% conversion on a *personal, in-person ask with a link handed over*, that is roughly **150 asks** — spread over a year, and every one of them attached to a job you were doing anyway. At 3–5 a month you pass 50 inside a year; a good parade season can deliver a month's worth in a fortnight.

**What one ask is worth.** Nothing else on the list moves local ranking as reliably per minute spent. A review is also the only marketing asset that keeps working for years and costs nothing to produce.

---

## 1. The prerequisite — get the two short links (fifteen minutes, once)

Nobody will search for you, find the profile and scroll to the review box. **The ask has to end with a link or a QR code in their hand**, or the conversion rate collapses to near zero.

In the Google Business Profile dashboard, for **each** of the two locations: **Read reviews → Get more reviews → Share review form.** That produces a short `g.page/r/…` link. Copy both somewhere permanent.

Then generate a **QR code** for each (any free generator) and get them printed. This is the whole infrastructure.

### Which profile gets which customer

Two profiles split the review count and neither builds authority. Be deliberate:

| Customer | Profile |
|---|---|
| Walk-in and shop customers, anything collected from Pindarou | **Store** — the profile that needs it. It carries the public 4.6 |
| Anything collected from or delivered by the Kaimakli factory, big commissions | **Factory** |
| Unsure | **Store** |

Default to the store. It has both the higher visibility and the weaker count.

## 2. Put the link where it costs nothing to be

Each of these is set up once and then works permanently, with no habit attached:

- **Invoice and delivery-note footer** — QR code plus one line: *«Πείτε μας τη γνώμη σας»*. Reprinting stationery once buys you a permanent, silent ask on every job.
- **A small standing card at the Pindarou counter** — QR code, big, facing the customer.
- **The workshop door / collection point** at Kaimakli.
- **Email signature** on both addresses.
- **The website** — a "leave a review" link on the contact page. *I can add this whenever you want; say the word and it ships.*
- **WhatsApp Business quick reply** — save the message from §4 as a canned reply so sending it is two taps.

## 3. The three moments that actually convert

Timing matters more than wording. Ask when the customer is holding the thing and pleased with it.

1. **At handover** — collection or delivery. The single best moment, and a verbal ask beats any message. Hand over the QR card as you hand over the flag.
2. **The day after a parade or a feast day** — the school or church has just used the λάβαρο in front of a crowd and been complimented on it. This is the strongest emotional moment in the whole cycle and it is currently going completely unused.
3. **When a customer says something nice unprompted** — on the phone, at the counter, in an email. *Every* such remark should be answered with "would you mind putting that on Google?" That sentence, used consistently, is most of the 36.

## 5. The seasonal batch — the biggest single unlock

**This business's seasonality is an advantage here and it is being wasted.** In the week after 28 October, several dozen schools have simultaneously just paraded behind a banner you made. They are all pleased at the same time, about the same thing.

**Four times a year — the first week of April, the first week of November, and after 25 March and 1 October — send the WhatsApp message to every customer from that season's order book.** One sitting, an hour, maybe forty messages. At 30% that is a dozen reviews in a week, and they will be specific and enthusiastic because the parade just happened.

Two rules: send them individually rather than as a broadcast list, and **spread them over several days**. Forty identical messages producing forty reviews in one afternoon looks to Google exactly like a review-buying pattern, and they get filtered.

## 6. The back catalogue — one legitimate one-off

Decades of customers exist and nothing prohibits asking a past customer for a review. **Pick 30–40 from the last two or three years that you remember going well** — schools, parishes, clubs where you know the person by name — and work through them over a month, a few a day. Phone is better than message for these.

Expect a lower rate than fresh jobs, but it is a one-off that could produce 8–12 reviews from work already done and paid for.

## 7. Getting the right words into them

A review saying "excellent service" ranks nothing. A review saying *«κεντητό λάβαρο για το σχολείο μας»* helps that page rank for that query.

Never dictate the wording — Google filters templated reviews and customers resent being scripted. Instead, prompt naturally at the end of the ask:

> «Αν μπορείτε, αναφέρετε τι σας φτιάξαμε — βοηθάει άλλα σχολεία να μας βρουν.»

Across fifty reviews that one sentence puts λάβαρα, σημαίες, κεντήματα, σχολείο, εκκλησία and Λευκωσία into your profile in customers' own words. **That is the closest thing to free ranking signal that exists.**

## 8. Reply to every single one

Two minutes each, in the language the review was written in, mentioning the product:

> «Σας ευχαριστούμε πολύ! Χαιρόμαστε που το λάβαρο του σχολείου σας άρεσε. Καλή επιτυχία στην παρέλαση!»

Replies are a confirmed ranking signal, they add your keywords to the profile, and — the part that matters most — **a profile where the owner answers everyone visibly encourages the next person to write one.**

**Bad reviews:** reply calmly, factually, publicly, within a day, and offer to fix it. A well-handled complaint reads better to a prospective customer than an unbroken wall of five stars, which reads as fake. Do not ask Google to remove anything unless it breaches policy.

## 9. What will get you filtered or suspended

Volume gained the wrong way is worse than no volume — Google removes reviews retroactively and can suspend a profile.

- **Never offer a discount, a gift or a prize for a review.** Explicitly against Google policy, and it is the most common way small businesses get their reviews wiped.
- **Never ask only the happy ones ("gating").** Ask everyone; the rating is 4.6 and 5.0 for a reason.
- **Never write them yourself, and do not have staff or family write them.** Google matches devices, IPs and account patterns. These get caught, and they poison the profile.
- **Never let customers leave reviews on the shop's own wifi or a shop device.** Same-IP clusters look manufactured. Let them do it on their own phone, on their own data, later.
- **Never buy reviews.** Cyprus agencies do sell them. They are detected, and the cleanup is worse than the gap.
- Expect **10–20% attrition** anyway on entirely legitimate reviews. That is normal; do not chase individual disappearances.

## 10. Tracking it

One number, checked monthly in the Google Business Profile dashboard: **reviews on each profile.** Log it. If a month adds fewer than three, the asking stopped — that is the only diagnosis needed.

Rough trajectory, if the ask is attached to every job and the seasonal batches happen:

| | Store | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Today | 14 | |
| +3 months | ~25 | includes the back-catalogue push and the October batch |
| +6 months | ~35 | |
| +12 months | **50+** | past Laser Signs' 60 within about eighteen months |

Hercules' 192 took years and is not the target. **50 genuine reviews at 4.6+ in Nicosia is enough to be very hard to displace.**
