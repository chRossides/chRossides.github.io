# Keyword list for Google Ads Keyword Planner

Built from the product range confirmed in [PRODUCT-SPECS.md](PRODUCT-SPECS.md) and the search vocabulary in [profile.md](profile.md). **262 unique terms** across Greek, English and Greeklish, grouped by the page each one maps to.

## Before you start

**Location is now the only control that matters.** Google has removed the language selector from Keyword Planner in current accounts, so you cannot split Greek from English any more. Set **Τοποθεσίες / Locations: Κύπρος** and nothing else. Leave it on a default and you will get Greek-market volume, which is roughly forty times the population and will send you after the wrong terms.

Because language cannot be separated, run **everything in one paste** — the single block at the bottom of this page. Greek, English and Greeklish terms all return their own volumes regardless of the interface language.

**Expect small numbers, and do not dismiss them.** Cyprus has under a million people and this is a niche trade. **10–100 searches a month is genuinely good** here. Anything showing 1,000+ deserves suspicion: it usually means the location filter slipped, or the term is ambiguous and picking up unrelated intent.

**Without an active campaign you get ranges**, not exact figures. That is enough — you only need to rank terms against each other.

**Networks: Google only.** Not "Google και συνεργάτες αναζήτησης", which inflates everything.

## What to do with the numbers

Sort by volume, then read each term against three questions:

1. **Does a page already target it?** Most do. A high-volume term with no page is the next page to build.
2. **Is the intent commercial?** «κυπριακή σημαία» is mostly people wanting a picture of the flag; «αγορά σημαίας» is someone with a wallet out. Volume without intent is the `flags cyprus` trap — dominated by Wikipedia and stock-photo sites.
3. **Is it seasonal?** Parade terms spike around 25 March, 1 April, 1 October and 28 October. Click a keyword to see the monthly breakdown before judging an annual average.

Watch the **Greeklish and accent-free rows** in particular. If «lavara sxoleiou» carries real volume, that is a signal about how Cypriots actually type on a phone, and it would justify mentioning those spellings in page copy.

The **military block has no baseline at all** — it has never been researched, and three directories already categorise the business that way. That is where the numbers are most likely to surprise you.

Bring the CSV back and I will map it against the current pages and say where the gaps are.

**A note on paid search.** This list is for *research*, not necessarily for spending. With no published prices and a phone-only sales process, paid clicks convert worse than they would for a competitor with a checkout. The volumes are most useful for deciding what to write next.

## Read this before the numbers

**61 of 263 terms returned volume. 202 returned nothing.** Everything that did return is bucketed into **5,000 / 500 / 50** — there is no finer grain — and the monthly breakdown columns came back empty.

That is what Keyword Planner gives an account with no active spend. **"No data" means below the reporting floor or suppressed, not "nobody searches this."** Several of the Phase 2 gap pages target exactly the kind of low-volume, high-intent query that Google suppresses. Treat all of this as a coarse ranking, not a measurement.

## What the data changed

**Military supplies is the find.** «στρατιωτικα ειδη» alone returns **500/month**, joint second-highest in the set, and eighteen military terms carry volume — πηλήκια, άρβυλα, μπερέδες, στρατιωτικά ρούχα, army shop nicosia, army boots cyprus. The page was built on a hunch from three directory categorisations; the numbers back it. Splitting it into boots and headwear pages was considered and **declined by the owner on 3 August 2026** — it is not a line the workshop wants to lead on, so the single page stays.

**Repairs has no search demand.** All seven terms returned zero. The page was previously ranked as a top opportunity on the grounds of "zero competition, high intent" — the competition is indeed zero, but so is the query volume. It keeps its place as a differentiator for someone already reading the banner pages, and as a reason for a parish to call, but it will not bring traffic and should not be prioritised as though it will.

**Greeklish is real.** Thirteen Greeklish terms carry volume: `simaies`, `lavara`, `pilikia`, `arvila`, `mperedes`, `diakritika`, `kentimata`, `epomides`, `telamonas`, `sakidio platis`, `stratiotika eidi`, `stratiotika arvila`, `agora simaias`. Most striking, **«στρατιωτικα ειδη» unaccented returns 500 against 50 for the accented form** — ten to one. With 93% of forecast impressions on mobile, that fits: people do not reach for accents on a phone keyboard.

Do **not** contort the copy to chase this. Google handles transliteration and accent-stripping well enough, and unaccented Greek in body text reads as illiterate. It is a fact about the customers, not an instruction about the writing.

**Competition is low almost everywhere** — 39 terms "Χαμηλός", 2 "Υψηλός", 1 "Μέτριος". Cheap to advertise, if that is ever wanted.

## Two cautions

**«flags cyprus» at 5,000/month is ten times everything else, and still the wrong target.** What ranks for it: Wikipedia three times, Amazon, Getty Images, two US retailers. It is people wanting a picture of the flag. High volume, near-zero commercial intent.

**Volume is not revenue.** One school λάβαρο with pole and harness is worth many pairs of boots. Military carries three times the search volume of banners at a fraction of the order value. Do not let this table reorder the priorities on its own.
