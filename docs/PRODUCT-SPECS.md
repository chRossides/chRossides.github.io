Hercules publishes 140 gsm. At 120 gsm ours is lighter, so **weight is not a claim to lead with** — but stating it is still better than their competitors' silence, and lighter fabric flies better in light wind, which is a legitimate framing.

### The largest flag ever made

**58 m × 27 m**, commissioned by an APOEL supporter. A special job: **15 rolls of fabric, three full days of work**. It was too large to unroll inside the factory — the first time it was ever opened out was on a football pitch, to confirm it had been assembled correctly.

⚠️ **This does not cleanly match the giant-flag research in [profile.md](profile.md).** 58 × 27 m is **1,566 m²**, against the **1,650 m²** the 2014 press reported. The cost given here — **around £3,000 Cyprus pounds** — is in a currency withdrawn in January 2008, while the press reported roughly **€4,000** for a 2014 event. Either the owner is recalling approximate figures in old money out of habit, or **this is a different and earlier flag than the one in the 2014 coverage**. Worth one clarifying question, because "the workshop that made the 1,650 m² flag" is the strongest single asset in the dossier and it should rest on figures that agree. Note also that the press figures came from the organiser, Τορναρίτης, not from the workshop — so the family's numbers may well be the more accurate ones.

## C. Finishing and fittings

**Flags**
- Polyester header.
- **No eyelets**, deliberately — to avoid corrosion. ⚠️ *Then how is the flag fixed to the pole? Not answered.*
- Reinforced, double-stitched hem.
- Optional fringing (κρόσσια).

**Λάβαρα poles**
- Aluminium, **39 mm diameter, 3 m long, 2–2.5 kg**.
- Optional **splitting mechanism** — the pole comes apart into two halves for transport and storage.
- Greek: blue ribbon and a **PVC cross**.
- Cypriot: white, with a **plastic or bronze sphere** — not truly spherical, a πόμωλο (knob).

**Carrying harness (τελαμώνες)**
- Cow leather.
- Two straps, **100 cm and 60 cm**, **4 cm** wide.
- Adjustable, with a non-corrosive buckle.

Poles and fittings are **sold separately**, on their own.

## Superseded rules

Confirmed by the owner on 2 August 2026. These three earlier instructions no longer apply:

1. **Sewn flags exist.** Previously recorded as digital print on polyester only. The **Greek flag is available sewn in every standard size**, in polyester. A4's flat "no" is superseded by B1's specific statement.
2. **Banners may be described as embroidered.** The earlier rule was *handmade velvet only* — the embroidery claim had been removed twice at the owner's request. Now correct: **appliqué embroidery for lettering**, with the central emblem printed or embroidered by choice.
3. **Lead times may be published** as specific figures. The earlier rule was *no fixed claims, always given with the quote*. The D table is now publishable copy.

Also settled: **delivery over €150 carries no charge** and may be stated. Prefer "χωρίς χρέωση" / "included" over δωρεάν, which is still avoided for quotes and advice.

**Size convention: width × height** (150 × 90), as the owner writes them. Label it explicitly wherever sizes appear — the reverse 90 × 150 form is equally common and a reader cannot tell the two apart.

## Minor gaps — none blocking

Worth picking up on the next call, but Phase 1 can be written without them.

1. **120 gsm** — the owner's own question mark. Since it is *below* Hercules' published 140, we are not leading with it anyway; the site currently describes the fabric without a number.
2. **Flag fixing** — with no eyelets, what attaches the flag to the pole? Sleeve, ties, rope and toggle?
3. **Giant flag** — 58 × 27 m is 1,566 m² against the press's 1,650 m², and the cost was given in a pre-2008 currency. Use the family's dimensions as primary and attribute the press figure separately; no price is published either way.
4. **Pole ribbon** — confirm "blue ribbon" / "white" means a spiral wrap along the pole rather than a hanging ribbon.
5. **Velvet weight** — asked, not answered.

*Resolved 2 August 2026: the "5000 × 3000" standard size is **5 m × 3 m**, i.e. 500 × 300 cm, as published.*
