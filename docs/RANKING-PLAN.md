# Plan to outrank the competition

Based on the competitor teardown and search-results analysis in [profile.md](profile.md), verified 26–27 July 2026.

**The strategic reading in one paragraph.** There is only one strong Cypriot competitor — Hercules Group in Limassol — and it publishes **nothing in Greek**. The one rival with a real audience in our own neighbourhood, S&H Laser Signs, has 3,690 Instagram followers pointing at a **67-word** flags page. Two Kaimakli names are entirely offline. Meanwhile the queries with clear commercial intent and *no* competent page anywhere — school banners, σημαιοστολισμός, table flags — are exactly the products we make and Greek importers cannot ship cheaply. **So the winning position is: own Greek-language, made-to-order, culturally specific flag work in Cyprus.** Do not fight Greek e-commerce on commodity national flags, where they are strongest and we are weakest.

---

## Phase 1 — Deepen what already exists (biggest return, no new pages)

Our product pages run about **190 words**. Hercules' run 638 and state fabric weight and lead time. That single gap explains most of the ranking difference, and it is the cheapest thing to fix.

For each of the eight category pages, add:

1. **Materials** — fabric type and weight (Hercules says "140 gsm polyester"; we should be at least as specific), velvet weight for λάβαρα, thread and backing for embroidery.
2. **Sizes** — a real size table with the common dimensions and what each is used for (90×150 flagpole, school parade banner, desk flag).
3. **Lead times** — Hercules publishes **21 days**. If we can beat that locally, saying so attacks their weakest published claim head-on. *Needs your input.*
4. **Options and finishing** — pole pockets, eyelets, rope and toggle, fringing, cross vs sphere finial.
5. **"No minimum order"** stated explicitly on every page. Sapo demands 300 pieces; nobody else states a minimum at all. This is a verified, stateable differentiator.
6. **A guide price — "from €X"** — if you are willing. Only three competitors publish flag prices, and **not one publishes anything for bespoke sewn or embroidered work**; Hercules hides all of it behind "Price on Request". A published starting price would be unique in Cyprus. *Needs your decision.*

**Target: 600+ words per category page**, written for customers rather than for search engines.

## Phase 3 — Authority content

1. **The 1,650 m² flag page.** Concrete, numerical, unrepeatable by any competitor. Needs a photograph — see the evidence checklist in profile.md.
2. **"Our story" / 1938.** The site's biggest content gap. Three generations, still handmade. Also the press hook.
3. **Seasonal articles**, published 4–6 weeks before each parade season (25 March, 1 April, 1 October, 28 October) — e.g. "what a school needs for the 25 March parade". Hercules has an active blog; we have none.

## Phase 4 — Fix the signals feeding all of it

The site is now the fastest and best-structured in the market, but **nothing feeds it**.

1. **Reviews: 14 → 50+.** Hercules has 192, Laser Signs 60. This is the single biggest ranking factor still available. Ask every satisfied customer. Reviews naming products ("school banner", "church λάβαρο") also help those pages rank. Steer them to the **store** profile — the factory profile has one review.
2. **Restart social — see [SOCIAL.md](SOCIAL.md).** Facebook has been silent since **March 2023**; Instagram has **76 followers** against Laser Signs' 3,690. A flag workshop generates exactly the finished-work photography that performs on those channels. The restart sequence and the capture-to-publish pipeline that stops it dying again are set out in full in SOCIAL.md.
3. **Google Business Profiles** — add the store's opening hours, change its category from the generic "Store", and add photos to both. *(Both profiles already point correctly at chrossides.com and are rated 4.6 and 5.0 — verified.)*
4. **Directory listings** — see [OFFSITE.md](OFFSITE.md). Note that **Sapo and Pafitis are both listed under Flags & Banners but sell no flags**; their directory traffic is takeable.

## Sequencing

**Now:** Phase 1 (deepen existing pages) and the reviews push in Phase 4 — highest return, and Phase 1 needs only your answers on lead times and prices.
**Next 1–2 months:** Phase 2 gap pages, starting with **λάβαρα σχολείων**, timed to land well before the 1 October and 28 October parades.
**Ongoing:** Phase 3 story content and the social restart.

## What I need from you to start

1. **Lead times** — realistic turnaround for a printed flag, a custom logo flag, an embroidered banner.
2. **Prices** — are you willing to publish "from €X" figures? If not, we compete on everything else, but this is a genuine gap in the market.
3. **Fabrics** — what polyester weight do you print on, and what velvet do you use for λάβαρα?
4. **Sizes** — your standard flag and banner dimensions.
5. **Photographs** — finished work, ideally including the giant flag.

Give me 1, 3 and 4 and I can write all of Phase 1 immediately.

## How we will know it is working

Track in Search Console (Performance → Queries), not by guessing:

- Impressions and average position for the target Greek queries above.
- Clicks on `/proionta/` pages versus the homepage.
- **Local pack visibility** — searches for "σημαίες Λευκωσία" and similar from a phone in Nicosia. The US-based tooling used for this research cannot see the local pack, and for these queries it is probably the most important surface of all.

Reasonable expectation: gap-page rankings within **2–3 months** given how weak the competition is on those terms; commodity national-flag queries may never be won, and that is fine — they are the wrong customers anyway.
