# Photography brief

What to shoot, in priority order, and the technical specification the site needs. Photographs are the only outstanding ask on the whole project — five product pages currently have no image at all, and two more use a picture of a printing machine to illustrate a finished product.

## Technical specification — applies to every shot

| | |
|---|---|
| **Orientation** | **Landscape.** The page renders images at 760 px wide; a landscape frame fills it, a portrait one is capped at 560 px tall and sits small. Shoot portrait only where the object demands it (a hanging banner) |
| **Aspect ratio** | 3:2 is ideal. The site never crops, so whatever is shot is what appears |
| **Resolution** | **2400 px on the long edge minimum.** Deliver the camera originals, not resized copies |
| **Format** | JPEG straight from the camera. No filters, no heavy retouching, no watermarks |
| **Background** | **Plain and uncluttered** — a white or grey wall, or a plain sheet hung behind. This is the single biggest problem with the current photographs: shelving, plastic crates and ceiling beams sit right beside the subject, and no amount of editing fixes it |
| **Light** | Even daylight. Near a window or outside in shade. Avoid direct sun, camera flash, and mixed indoor lighting, which turns velvet muddy |
| **Framing** | Fill the frame with the subject and leave a little air. Shoot each item twice: the **whole object**, then a **close detail** of the workmanship |
| **Scale** | For large items, include something that conveys size |

A phone camera is fine. **93% of visitors arrive on a phone**, where these render about 340 px wide — clarity and a clean background matter far more than equipment.

One thing to avoid: **no price tags or price lists visible in any shot.** The site publishes no prices and that is a settled decision.

## Priority 1 — pages that currently have no image at all

These five are the whole reason for the shoot.

**1. Table flags** — `/proionta/epitrapezies-simaies/`
A table flag on its silver base, on a plain desk. Ideally a group of three or four different countries together, which shows the range in one frame. A second shot from lower down, at desk-eye level.

**2. Flag poles and fittings** — `/proionta/istoi-kontaria/`
This page describes six things and shows none of them:
- The three pole finishes side by side — blue and white, plain white, velvet with the yellow rope
- The finials: PVC cross, plastic sphere, bronze πόμωλο — grouped together on a plain surface
- The leather harness, laid flat so both straps and the buckle are visible
- One pole shown split at the joining mechanism

**3. Bunting / σημαιοστολισμός** — `/proionta/simaiostolismos/`
A run of bunting actually strung up, so the spacing reads — three flags per metre is the whole selling point. Outdoors against sky is ideal. A second shot of a folded run in the hand, showing the flags are properly hemmed.

**4. Banner repairs** — `/proionta/episkevi-lavaron/`
The most persuasive shot on the list if it can be arranged: **a worn banner before and after**. Failing that, a close detail of new gold fringing being attached, or a tired old banner alongside fresh fringing and tassels ready to go on.

**5. Military supplies** — `/proionta/stratiotika-eidi/`
The kepi photograph is good and stays. What is missing is the rest of the page: berets and jockey caps, boots, a backpack, water bottles and torch. One group shot of the field equipment together, plus headwear on its own.

## Priority 2 — replacing photographs that misrepresent the product

**6. Banners, reshot properly** — the parade and school/church pages
The two existing banner photographs are the best content on the site and the worst photographs. Same banners, hung against a **plain wall or a sheet**, in daylight. Portrait is correct here — the site places two portrait banners side by side. Then a **close detail** of the appliqué lettering, the gold fringing and a tassel, which is where the handwork actually shows.

**7. Finished national flags** — `/proionta/ethnikes-simaies/`
The page currently shows a printing machine. It needs finished flags: one flying on a pole outdoors, and a folded stack showing the reinforced double-stitched hem and the polyester header — the construction details the copy describes but nothing illustrates.

**8. A finished logo flag** — `/proionta/simaies-me-logotypo/`
Same problem, same fix. A finished custom flag, ideally flying, showing a printed logo crisply.

## Priority 3 — the story pages, not yet written

**9. The workshop at work** — hands cutting fabric, someone at the sewing machine, the embroidery machine mid-run with thread moving. These carry the "handmade in Kaimakli since 1938" claim that the whole site rests on, and there is currently one generic workshop photograph doing that job.

**10. The shopfront** — the sign reads ΧΑΡ. ΡΩΣΣΙΔΗΣ ΛΤΔ with ΕΤΟΣ ΙΔΡΥΣΕΩΣ 1938, the website and the phone number. There is a usable 2018 photograph on the Google listing; a clean modern one in good light would serve the "our story" page and the social profiles.

**11. The giant flag** — if any photograph exists in the family's own records, of the 58 × 27 m flag either in the workshop or opened out. This is the single most valuable image on the list and the one nobody can reshoot.

## If only an hour is available

Priorities 1 and 6. That is table flags, poles and fittings, bunting, a repair, the military equipment, and the banners reshot against a plain background — six setups, all of them small objects that can be carried to one well-lit spot with a sheet behind them.

That alone would put a real photograph on every product page on the site.
