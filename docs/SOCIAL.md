# Restarting social — and keeping it alive

Expands Phase 4.2 of [RANKING-PLAN.md](RANKING-PLAN.md). Facts drawn from [profile.md](profile.md) §4, verified 26–27 July 2026.

**Where we stand.** Facebook: one page, ~580 followers, **last post March 2023**. Instagram: `chrossides_ltd`, **76 followers**. S&H Laser Signs, two streets away in Kaimakli, has 3,690 Instagram followers and an active TikTok — pointing at a 67-word flags page.

**The honest diagnosis.** The account did not die from lack of ideas. A workshop that hand-embroiders λάβαρα generates more publishable material in a week than most businesses manage in a year. It died because posting depended on one busy person deciding, photographing, writing and publishing all in the same moment. **Everything below is designed to break that single step into four cheap ones that different people can do at different times.** If we only fix the posting and not the pipeline, it stops again by Christmas.

**Why start now and not in September.** 1 October and 28 October are two of the four demand peaks. An account that has been posting steadily since August walks into that season with reach; an account revived in week one of September does not. Starting this week buys roughly ten weeks of runway.

---

## Read this first — social is not the priority, and you may reasonably skip it

Parts 1–3 below describe social done properly. **If the capacity is not there, do not do a diluted version of it — do something else instead.** Half-hearted posting costs real time and returns almost nothing, and this business has cheaper wins sitting untouched.

**The opportunity cost of skipping social is genuinely low.** Our customers arrive through search and through Google Maps, not through a feed. The best evidence for this is the competitor we keep comparing ourselves to: **S&H Laser Signs has 3,690 Instagram followers pointing at a 67-word flags page with no prices and no H1.** All that audience, and nothing to convert it on. Their followers are not obviously buying flags. Meanwhile Hercules Group — the one genuinely strong competitor — wins on **3,034 indexed pages and 192 reviews**, not on social.

### Where an hour actually returns most, in order

| | Effort | Why it beats social |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Reviews — 14 → 50+** | **Zero preparation.** One sentence at the counter, during a conversation that is happening anyway | The single biggest ranking factor still available to us. Hercules has 192. Reviews naming products ("λάβαρο σχολείου") also lift those pages |
| **2. Google Business Profile photos** | **One afternoon, once, never repeated** | Both profiles have **one photo each**. Photos are the main thing people judge in the local pack, and the local pack is probably our most important surface of all |
| **3. Answering the five questions in RANKING-PLAN.md** | **One phone call** — lead times, fabrics, sizes | I turn that call into ~3,000 words across eight product pages. Your hour, my week. This is the biggest ranking gap we have |
| **4. Store opening hours + category fix** | **Ten minutes** | Currently published nowhere at all |
| **5. Social** | Ongoing, forever | Last on the list, and it is not close |

Items 1–4 are all **one-off or reflexive**. None of them needs a habit, a calendar or a spare evening. Together they are worth more than a year of Instagram.

### The one habit worth keeping even if you never post

**Take the photos anyway.**

Three photos of every job before it is folded, into one WhatsApp group. Even if nothing is ever posted, that library is the raw material for the website's product pages, for the Google profiles, and for the press pitch — **none of which need cadence.** A photo taken today is just as useful on a product page in six months. A post not made today is gone.

This is the part to protect. The publishing can wait indefinitely; the photography cannot, because the flag ships and the moment is gone.

### If you want *some* social presence for near-zero cost

Three descending options. Pick one and ignore the rest of this document.

**(a) Four posts a year — about two hours, total.** One post in each parade run-up: mid-February, mid-August, mid-September, and one in spring. Each is a photo plus three sentences plus an "order by" date. Batched, scheduled in advance, forgotten. Four posts a year is not a growth strategy, but the page stops reading as abandoned and it lands in the only four windows that matter commercially.

**(b) Make it not look dead, then walk away.** Half an hour, once: add the website link, both addresses, phone, opening hours and a WhatsApp button to the Facebook page, refresh the cover photo, and stop. Roughly 580 people already follow that page and some of them look you up before phoning. **A page with correct information and an old last post still does that job.** A page with a dead link does not. This is the floor, and it is a legitimate place to stop.

**(c) Split the work so it never touches you.** The bench takes photos (no marginal cost — see above). Someone else does the desk half. Realistic candidates: whoever staffs the Pindarou counter during quiet hours, a younger family member, or a local freelancer at roughly €100–150 a month — about the value of one banner order. **If you give me the photos, I can write the captions and prepare a month at a time**; publishing is then a ten-minute job for whoever holds the account.

**My recommendation: (b) now, plus the photo habit, and revisit (a) or (c) in August.** Then put the freed-up effort into reviews and into answering the five product questions — that is where the ranking actually moves.

Everything below assumes you have chosen to invest properly. It is here for when that is true, not as a reproach if it never is.

## Part 1 — The restart (about six weeks)

### Week 0 — Fix the furniture before posting anything

Half an hour of admin that makes every later post work harder.

1. **Facebook page settings** — add website `chrossides.com`, both addresses, phone, opening hours, and a **Call** or **WhatsApp** button. The page currently sits on a `profile.php?id=100052710947198` URL; claim a proper username (`facebook.com/chrossides` or similar) so it is linkable and memorable.
2. **Instagram bio** — Greek first, one line on what we make, city, and the site link. Switch to a **Professional / Business** account if it is not already; this is what unlocks scheduling and the insights we need in Part 3.
3. **Link the two in Meta Business Suite** so one composer publishes to both. This single step is what makes the monthly batch session in Part 2 possible.
4. **Consistent name** — use `Charalambos Rossides Ltd / Χαράλαμπος Ρωσσίδης ΛΤΔ` exactly as in [OFFSITE.md](OFFSITE.md). Five variants are already in circulation and they stop search engines resolving us to one business.

### Weeks 1–6 — The relaunch sequence

No apology post, no "we're back". A dormant page that starts posting good work simply reads as a page that posts good work.

| # | Post | Why it leads |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The giant flag.** 1,650 m², 280 kg, raised over Nicosia in 2014 and unfurled across the GSP south stand. | The strongest asset we own and unrepeatable by any competitor. Use the safe wording from profile.md — *specific and factual*, not "largest in the world". Needs a photograph. |
| 2 | **Hands at work** — a close-up of gold thread going into a λάβαρο. | Craft process outperforms finished-product photography everywhere, and **no Cypriot competitor posts any**. |
| 3 | **Three generations / 1938.** One old photograph, three sentences. | The story nobody has told. Also the press hook. |
| 4 | **A finished school λάβαρο** leaving the workshop. | Seeds the product we most want to rank for before parade season. |
| 5 | **Scale of the range** — a wall of national flags, or a stack of finished orders. | Answers "can they handle my job?" without a word of copy. |
| 6 | **The 1 October / 28 October deadline post.** "Order by [date] for the parade." | Converts. Repeat, reworded, every two weeks through September. |

Two posts a week through this stretch. **Do not wait for new photography** — six weeks of relaunch content can come entirely out of what is already on phones and in the office drawer.

### What not to do yet

- **No TikTok.** Laser Signs' 3,690 followers came from years of consistency. Two channels done properly beat three abandoned ones, and Meta Business Suite runs both of ours from one screen.
- **No paid ads** until the page has eight weeks of organic posts to land on.
- **No follower-count target.** See Part 3 for what to measure instead.

## Part 2 — The pipeline that keeps feeding them

Five stages. Only the first happens daily, and it takes under a minute.

### 1. Capture — at the bench, by whoever made it

> **The rule: nothing leaves the workshop unphotographed.** Three photos and one short video of every job, before it is folded.

- Phone camera, daylight, no editing, no judgement about whether it is "good enough". Volume at capture is what makes selection possible later.
- Photograph the **making**, not just the finished piece: the needle in the velvet, the printer running, the cutting table, the pile of finials. This is the material competitors cannot fake.
- Send straight into **one WhatsApp group** — shop, factory, and whoever schedules. That is the whole obligation. Nobody at the bench ever writes a caption or opens Instagram.

This is the step that failed last time. It only survives if it costs the person holding the flag nothing.

### 2. Store — one shared library

- A shared **Google Drive or Google Photos** album, one folder per month, files named `YYYY-MM-DD_product_customer`.
- Empty the WhatsApp group into it weekly — WhatsApp compresses and eventually deletes, so it is a courier, not an archive.
- **This library is not only for social.** It feeds the website's product pages, the Google Business Profile photos (both profiles currently have **one photo each**), and the press pitch. Every photo taken earns its keep three or four times.

### 3. Queue — a spreadsheet, nothing fancier

Columns: `photo · product · Greek caption · English caption · page it links to · status · scheduled date`.

Never post from a blank page. Every post comes from one of six standing buckets, so the question is never "what shall we post?" but "which bucket is thinnest?":

| Bucket | Example | Links to |
|---|---|---|
| **Process** | Gold embroidery close-up, stitch by stitch | `/proionta/lavara` |
| **Finished job** | A banner going out the door | the matching product page |
| **Scale & spec** | "1,650 m². 280 kg. Twelve people, six hours." | the story page |
| **Seasonal** | "Six weeks to 28 October — school orders open" | λάβαρα σχολείων |
| **Heritage** | An old workshop photograph | "Our story" |
| **Practical** | "Which fabric for an outdoor flag?" | the fabric guide |

The last column matters. **Every post should have somewhere on the site to send people** — and conversely, every new page from Phase 2 of the ranking plan should ship with three posts pointing at it.

### 4. Publish — one desk session a month

- **60–90 minutes, same day each month**, in Meta Business Suite → Planner. Schedule **8–12 posts** covering the next four weeks, to Facebook and Instagram together.
- Everything is written and queued in advance, so a busy fortnight in the workshop does not create a silent fortnight on the page.
- Leave two slots empty each month for anything genuinely timely — a big job, a delivery, a visit.
- **Cadence:** two posts a week normally; three or four in the six weeks before each parade date.

### 5. Feed back — close the loops

- **Every completed job → a review request.** Reviews are the biggest ranking factor still available to us (14 vs Hercules' 192). Steer them to the **store** profile, which has one.
- **Customer photos → repost.** Schools, churches and clubs photograph their own banners on parade day. Ask permission once, at order time, and that day's content writes itself.
- **Social photography → the website.** The best shots from the library go onto product pages, which is exactly what our 190-word pages need.
- **Anything that performs unusually well → a page.** If a post about church λάβαρα gets shared, that is free evidence about which page to write next.

### The seasonal spine

Four fixed dates drive the year. Work backwards from each:

| | 25 March | 1 April | 1 October | 28 October |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **6 weeks before** | mid-Feb | mid-Feb | mid-Aug | mid-Sep |
| Content starts | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ |
| **4 weeks before** — "order by" deadline posts | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ |
| **Week of** — parade photos, customer reposts | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ |

Between seasons, process and heritage content carries the account. **August and September are the year's most important posting months**, not the quiet ones.

## Part 3 — Knowing whether it is working

Follower count is the wrong number. It flatters slowly and says nothing about revenue. Track monthly, from Meta Business Suite Insights:

1. **Profile → website clicks** and **call / WhatsApp button taps.** These are the only social numbers that map to enquiries.
2. **Saves and shares**, especially on process posts — the signal that content is worth making more of.
3. **Google reviews gained per month.** The loop in stage 5 either works or it does not.
4. **Enquiries that mention Facebook or Instagram** — worth one line in the order book. Cheap, and more honest than any platform metric.

Realistic twelve-month expectation: Instagram from 76 to somewhere in the low hundreds, Facebook reach recovered on a page that already has 580 followers, and a photo library big enough to fix the website and both Google profiles. **Laser Signs' 3,690 is not the target** — their audience does not convert, because there is nothing to convert it on.

## What would kill it again — and the fix

| Failure | Fix already in the plan |
|---|---|
| One person owns everything | Capture, curation and publishing are three people's work, not one's |
| "The photo isn't good enough" | Volume at the bench, selection at the desk. Craft photography does not need to be polished |
| A busy fortnight = silence | A month scheduled in advance absorbs it |
| Nothing to say in January | Six standing buckets; process and heritage carry the off-season |
| Waiting for the perfect first post | Post the backlog. The relaunch needs no new photography |
