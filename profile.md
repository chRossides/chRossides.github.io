### The 1938 question — needs a decision

The website says "founded 1938" and "87 years". The registry tells a more complicated story:

- **1938** — heritage claim, on the company's own site and its Google listings. No registry record supports it, **but none contradicts it either**: Cyprus's pre-independence business-name registrations are largely not in the online register.
- **19 December 1969** — `ΧΑΡΑΛΑΜΠΟΣ ΡΩΣΣΙΔΗΣ & ΣΙΑ`, a general partnership, reg. **Σ 4063**, struck off 20 December 1986. **[confirmed]** that this entity existed; **[unverified]** that it is the predecessor of the Ltd, though the timing (struck off three years after the Ltd was incorporated) is the classic pattern of a family partnership incorporating.
- **29 January 1983** — the limited company. Directory site `listcompany.org` publishes "Year established: 1983", which almost certainly just mirrors this.

**These are reconcilable.** A business can trade from 1938 and incorporate in 1983. The safe wording — which we should adopt if anyone challenges the claim — is *"trading since 1938; incorporated as a limited company in 1983"*. **Action: ask the family to confirm the 1938 date and whether any document (photo, invoice, licence, press cutting) evidences it.** If they can produce one, it is both a credibility anchor and a PR asset.

## 3. Products and market position

**Product range** (from our own site and directory categories): national flags of any country and size, digitally printed logo flags on polyester, handmade velvet parade and church banners with pole/cross/sphere finials, embroidered badges, emblems and insignia, πηλήκια (kepis) and military accessories, table flags, bunting, sports flags, poles and fittings. Directory listings also file them under hunting and army goods, suggesting a retail side to the Pindarou shop that the website does not currently mention. **Worth asking whether the shop's army/hunting retail line should appear on the site at all** — it may be a meaningful revenue stream that is currently invisible online.

**Competitive landscape** — the Cypriot field is fragmented and weakly digitised. Most rivals are sign/print shops or promotional-gift houses that treat flags as a side line. Named competitors **[confirmed]**:

- **Ricami Studio Designs & Embroideries** (Kaimakli, `ricamistudio.com`, est. 1998) — the strongest digital competitor, with its own branded site and e-shop.
- **Classmates** (`classmates.com.cy`) — full e-commerce for army insignia, velcro badges, National Guard kit. Directly overlaps the embroidery/military line.
- **Sapo Giveaways Public Ltd** (est. 1988, three showrooms), **Hercules Group** (Limassol, e-shop), **Pantone Signs**, **Φώτης Παφίτης & Υιός**, **Αναστασίου Awards Center**, and a Facebook-only trader "Σημαίες Παραγγελίες Πωλήσεις".

**The bigger threat is Greek importers**, which dominate every Greek-language search: Kokkonis (127 years old), flags.gr, ekivotos.com, psomiadisflags.gr, hatzakis1938.gr. Several explicitly sell Cyprus flags and Cypriot team flags, and Skroutz/Jumbo carry Cyprus-flag SKUs. **[confirmed]**

**Our defensible wedge:** Rossides is the only Cyprus player found positioned on *heritage + genuinely handmade + church/parade banners + military insignia*, made locally with no minimum order. Nobody else combines those.

**Demand pattern** — sharply seasonal, around fixed national dates: **25 March** (Greek Independence, student parades), **1 April** (EOKA), **1 October** (Cyprus Independence), **28 October** (Ohi Day). Municipal σημαιοστολισμός is formally mandated on national days, and the Ministry of Education publishes parade arrangements for schools. **[confirmed]** Content and outreach should lead these dates by 4–6 weeks.

**Search vocabulary Cypriots actually use:** σημαίες, λάβαρα, κεντητά σήματα, χρυσοκέντητα λάβαρα, εξαπτέρυγα, διαφημιστικές σημαίες / beach flags, σημαίες ομάδων, πηλήκια, διακριτικά Εθνικής Φρουράς.

**Visible SEO whitespace:** for *church λάβαρα*, *χρυσοκέντητα λάβαρα*, *κεντητά σήματα* and *beach flags / διαφημιστικές σημαίες*, only Greek suppliers rank — no Cyprus company competes. **[confirmed]**

## 5. The biggest untapped asset: the story

**No article, interview, blog post or press feature about this business exists anywhere.** Searches across philenews, politis, Cyprus Mail, in-cyprus, reporter.com.cy, sigmalive, kathimerini.com.cy and Greek-language queries returned nothing — every "Ρωσσίδης" hit was a different person. **[confirmed]**

An 87-year-old family workshop with three generations, hand-embroidering church banners in Kaimakli, has never been written about. That is a genuine human-interest story sitting unused, and it is the one thing no competitor can copy. It is also the natural pitch to Cypriot media ahead of a national-day parade season.

Equally, **no named customer is publicly verifiable.** The site's "trusted by schools, churches, municipalities" is currently unsubstantiated. Permission to name even five real customers would be a step-change in credibility.

### The giant Greek flag — our most valuable untold story

Researched 26 July 2026; **the family confirms this is a single flag of ours, documented in the press on two separate occasions a decade apart.**

**Specification** (from the 2014 coverage) — **1,650 m², 280 kg**, cost around **€4,000**. For scale, that is larger than every other Greek flag found in this research, including the Guinness-registered one.

**Appearance 1 — Nicosia, Saturday 28 June 2014.** Raised over a building in the heart of Nicosia on the Saturday afternoon before Greece played Costa Rica in the World Cup last 16. A **12-person crew took about six hours** in extreme heat. Organised by **Χριστόφορος Τορναρίτης**, a former football administrator/agent, who is quoted giving the specification directly: *"τὸ ἐμβαδόν τῆς σημαίας εἶναι 1.650 τετραγωνικὰ μέτρα καὶ τὸ βάρος της 280 κιλά"*. Greek media described it as *"τη μεγαλύτερη ελληνική σημαία που κατασκευάστηκε ποτέ"* — the largest Greek flag ever constructed. **Neither article names the manufacturer.**

#### The attribution gap

**No published source names the manufacturer.** Nineteen searches across Greek and English media, club and ultras channels found no article crediting Charalambos Rossides Ltd. This is not evidence against the account — *no* giant-flag coverage anywhere names its maker; Cypriot media credit the organiser or the fans, never the workshop that sewed it. But it means the claim currently rests on family testimony alone.

**To make it publishable, obtain any one of:** a dated workshop photograph, the 2014 invoice or order record, or written confirmation from **Χριστόφορος Τορναρίτης** — a named, findable person who commissioned it and would know exactly who made it. A single phone call could convert this from testimony into a citable fact.

#### How to word it safely

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "We made the largest Greek flag in the world" | **Risky.** Contestable and unregistered. |
| "We made the flag the Greek press called the largest ever constructed" | **Safe** — attributes the superlative to the press, which did print it. |
| "We made the 1,650 m², 280 kg flag flown over Nicosia in 2014 and across the GSP stand in 2024" | **Safest and strongest.** Specific, verifiable, unrepeatable by any competitor. |

For context on why the bare superlative is risky, the competing documented flags are all *smaller*, but one is officially registered and credited elsewhere: Didymoteicho 2001, **593 m², Guinness-registered, credited to Damos** (a Greek manufacturer) — https://damos.gr/flag/?lang=en · Santorini 2021, 1,500 m² — https://www.in.gr/2021/03/22/greece/25i-martiou-terastia-elliniki-simaia-gia-rekor-gkines/ · Alexandroupoli 2024, 600 m² — https://www.in.gr/2024/05/13/greece/megalyteri-simaia-tis-elladas-tha-ypsothei-stin-aleksandroupoli-poios-o-logos/

#### Why this matters more than anything else in this dossier

This is the single strongest marketing asset the business has. It is concrete, numerical, photogenic, tied to two nationally significant occasions, and **impossible for any competitor to claim**. It answers "can they handle a big job?" more convincingly than any amount of copy. It belongs on the website, in the Google Business descriptions, and at the centre of the press pitch described above — a story that leads with "the workshop behind the 1,650 m² flag" gets picked up far more readily than "family business since 1938".
